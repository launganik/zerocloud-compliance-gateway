# Dual-use pre-screening procedure

> Demo knowledge base: illustrative content for the hackathon, not official Forto policy.

## What dual-use goods are

Dual-use goods have both civilian and military uses. Typical examples are drone and UAV parts (frames, flight controllers, motors for unmanned aircraft), satellite navigation receivers, certain integrated circuits and microcontrollers, high-performance materials such as carbon fibre structures, and machine tools. In the EU, controlled items are listed in Annex I of the Dual-Use Regulation (EU) 2021/821. Exporting a listed item can require an export licence.

## When a booking is held

The compliance gateway holds a booking before ELM automates it when the goods may be dual-use, when the HS code is on the control list, or when the email contains technical performance data or confidential contract terms. A hold is not a rejection: it means a compliance manager must look at the case first.

## Steps for the compliance manager

1. Check the goods description and HS codes against the control list and Annex I of Regulation (EU) 2021/821.
2. If the goods may be dual-use, ask the shipper for an end-use statement and the export licence status.
3. If the shipper confirms no licence is needed and the end use is civilian, approve the case and record why.
4. If a licence is needed but missing, or the end use is unclear, reject the case and follow up with the customer.
5. Every decision is recorded in the gateway's audit log with the control-list version.

## Drone and UAV components

Frames, flight controllers, brushless motors and navigation modules for unmanned aircraft are common dual-use risks, even when each part looks like a general electronic or mechanical item. Treat "unmanned aerial", "UAV" or "drone" in a goods description as a reason to request an end-use statement before booking.
